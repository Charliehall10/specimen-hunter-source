# IP and regulatory check: Fish On! (7 October 2026)

**This is preliminary desk research, not legal advice.** It is a starting point to take to a qualified
professional: a UK chartered trade mark attorney (CITMA) for the name, a patent attorney (CIPA) for the
mechanics, and a gambling regulatory solicitor for licensing. Searches were done online on the date above
and can miss things (some registers block automated searches; see "Still to check").

## 1. Name: "Fish On!" (HIGH RISK)

| Mark | Owner | Register | Goods/services | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FISH ON! | King Show Games, Inc. (Minnetonka, MN), a slot game developer | US, serial 86209456 (filed 3 Mar 2014) | Class 28: gaming machines / slot machines | Live, registered |
| FISH ON! | King Show Games, Inc. | US reg. 6391135 (serial 90029102), registered 15 Jun 2021, first use 9 Mar 2021 | Class 9: downloadable computer game software | Live, registered; no Madrid (international) extension listed |
| FISH ON! | 2WIN! Global, LLC | US, serial 90767944 | Class 41: entertainment | Live, registered |
| FISH ON (others) | various | US | beer, restaurants, clothing; several abandoned | Not gaming |

**Finding:** an established slot developer already owns and uses "FISH ON!" for slot machines and game
software in the US. Even if no UK registration exists, using the same name for a slot game is likely to
be seen as confusingly similar in the very market (B2B slot suppliers) you want to sell into, blocks the
US entirely, and would make any studio or aggregator reluctant to take the game.

**Recommendation:** do not use "Fish On!" commercially. Pick a new name before hosting the demo
publicly or pitching. Candidates checked on the US register with **no live identical marks found**:
"Three Rods", "Bite Alarm", "Specimen Hunter" (the original working title). None was found as an
existing slot title in a web search. Each still needs a UK IPO and EUIPO check (below).

## 2. Mechanics and patents (MEDIUM: crowded space, no blocking patent found)

- **Fishing + cash-value fish in free spins is a well-known, heavily used format.** Reel Time Gaming's
  Fishin' Frenzy (c. 2014) and Reel Kingdom/Pragmatic's Big Bass Bonanza series use a fisherman
  wild that *collects* fish cash values during free spins.
- **Fish On! differs:** there is no collector symbol (every fish on screen pays immediately), and the
  trigger is the "Three Rods" bite-alarm mechanic (one alarm on each of reels 2, 3, 4) with retriggers.
  Lean on those differences in any pitch, and keep the look clearly your own (it is: all art is original).
- **Closest patent found:** Aristocrat US11386753B2 ("Gaming machine and method for providing a feature
  game"; priority 2014, in force to 2035). Claim 1 requires *holding* prize-value symbols across
  re-spins and replacing the others (hold-and-respin). Fish On! pays fish immediately with no holding,
  so on a plain reading it does not appear to fall within claim 1. Other results (cumulative free games,
  collect buttons, symbol modification) are generic or older.
- Scatter-on-reels-2-3-4 triggers, 10 fixed lines and a wild that substitutes are long-standing,
  common slot design (strong prior art).

**Recommendation:** before a commercial deal, ask a patent attorney for a freedom-to-operate opinion
on the free-spins cash-fish mechanic in the target markets (UK, EU, US). Budget for it, or let the
licensing studio do it (they usually do their own checks).

Sources: [US11386753B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11386753B2/en),
[US7070501B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7070501B2/en),
[US20210049874A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20210049874A1/en),

## 4. Licensing: who needs what

- UKGC guidance: "Any gambling software used by a remote operating licensee must be manufactured,
  supplied, installed or adapted by the holder of a gambling software licence."
- **Open question for a gambling solicitor:** whether you need your own gambling software licence if a
  licensed studio takes your game and supplies it under its licence (e.g. you are paid to design it, or
  you assign/licence the IP to them). The guidance does not answer this directly. Until you have advice,
  do not supply the game yourself to any operator, and never take real-money play.
- Taking real-money bets from GB players without an operating licence is a criminal offence.

Source: [UKGC: gambling software sector compliance](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/guide/gambling-software-and-online-businesses-sector-specific-compliance).

## 5. Other points

- **"Three Rods" / angling terms:** the dev log noted the EA rod licence (up to 3 rods). Using common angling
  words in a game is normal; just don't use the Environment Agency name or logo, or real tackle brands
  (none are used: the art is generic).
- **Fonts and code:** the game uses system fonts and open-source libraries (PixiJS: MIT; Electron: MIT;
  Vite: MIT). Keep their licence notices if you distribute the desktop app.
- **Previous voice line:** removed; no third-party audio remains.
