# Session 04 - 2026-10-12

## Goal
Polish Specimen Hunter to a marketable level: revamp the symbol art, and give the free spins their own way of
collecting fish (Charlie's idea: three swims covered before the feature starts; fish landing in them collect and
add a x2 multiplier each time).

## Decisions made
- **Baited swims.** Before the first free spin the three rods each cast a spod into a swim: 3 swims on 3 different
  reels, a random row on each, fixed for the whole feature. A fish landing in a swim pays its prize x the swim's
  multiplier, then the multiplier doubles: x2, x4, x8, x16, then x32 for every later catch. Fish outside the swims
  still pay x1 straight away, so the "every fish is weighed" identity stays.
- Swim positions are decided by the engine (server side), like every other outcome.
- **Rebalanced without touching the base game:** free-spins strips go from 4 to 3 Specimen Fish per reel and the
  2lb 4oz fish's draw weight goes from 300 to 394. Base strips are unchanged (base RTP still 54.14%), and so are
  the trigger rate and the feature length.
- **The win cap now matters.** A record fish in a x8 or deeper swim passes 5,000x, so the PAR now measures what the
  cap costs and the design RTP is the exact figure less that cost. The 5,000x max win is now actually reachable,
  which makes it advertisable.
- **Art direction:** tackle symbols sit straight on an underwater reel background with a sticker outline; fish and
  specials get coloured plates with gold rims, each symbol a different colour.

## Math results
- **Exact (uncapped): 96.226%** (base 54.140%, free spins 42.086%); trigger 1 in 151.70; E[free spins] 10.341;
  E[feature win] 63.85x, of which swims 26.18x; mean fish 2.8315x; expected catches per swim 0.9695 (matches
  spins x chance, a check on the step-through).
- **Cap cost:** 20,000,002 features with no cap: 1 in 39,841 pass 5,000x (largest 32,515x); cost 0.085% +/- 0.010.
- **Design RTP with the cap: 96.142%** (was 96.142% before the swims).
- Simulation, JS engine, 50M rounds (seed 2026): RTP 96.067% +/- 0.246, hit 25.49%, feature 1 in 151.7, SD 8.86,
  12 rounds capped. Python, 500M rounds: RTP 96.111% +/- 0.079, SD 9.06, 91 rounds capped (1 in 5.5M).
- Catches per swim per feature: 0: 36.4%, 1: 38.4%, 2: 18.5%, 3: 5.4%, 4: 1.1%, 5+: 0.2%. Swims pay 41% of the
  average feature win. 100x+ rounds 1 in 1,261 (was 1 in 1,407).
- PAR workbook recalculated with the `formulas` library: 1,734 cells, 0 errors; summary matches the engine exactly.

## Problems hit and lessons
- **Fish tails vanished on GPU-accelerated canvas** (headless Brave, SwiftShader) but not in software canvas:
  canvas shadows drawn under the large scale transform used for fish geometry wiped nearby pixels. Fixed by never
  using canvas shadows inside fish drawing; one drop shadow is applied when compositing at an identity transform.
  GPU and software renders now differ only by antialiasing. Lesson: compare GPU and CPU renders when canvas art
  looks wrong.
- First RTP check after adding the swims came in 0.1% low: it was the cap, which the old maths could ignore.
- Editing source while screenshots ran against the Vite dev server triggered a hot reload mid-run. Screenshots
  are now taken from a static build (`vite preview`).
